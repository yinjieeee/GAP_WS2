\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:summary}

This work establishes a topology-optimization-based inverse design platform for all-dielectric GaP nanostructures driven by monolayer WS$_2$ valley-polarized excitons, demonstrating systematic conversion of a circularly polarized point source into high-purity Laguerre--Gaussian (LG)
vortex beams for target modes LG$_{\ell,0}$, $\ell = 0$--$5$. Four principal findings are established:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{High-purity OAM mode generation across six topological charges:} Mode purity exceeds $94\%$ for $\ell \le 3$ (peak: $98.66\%$ at $\ell = 1$) and reaches $\sim\!82\%$ for $\ell = 4, 5$ within the $4.5\,\mu$m design domain; phase winding errors are below $1\%$ for all six designs (Table~\ref{tab:results_summary}).

\item \textbf{Topology-driven structural diversity:} The $\ell = 2$ design uniquely self-organizes into a two-arm SPP-like spiral, while all other designs converge to free-form non-intuitive geometries (Section~\ref{ssec:spiral_emergence}).

\item \textbf{Quantified valley polarization selectivity:} The $\ell = 3$ structure achieves a LCP/RCP purity contrast ratio exceeding $100{:}1$ ($94.13\%$ vs.\ $0.03\%$), providing a directly experimentally benchmarkable valley-selectivity baseline (Section~\ref{sec:pol_selectivity}).

\item \textbf{Near-to-far-field chirality conservation:} The near-field mixed-chirality state fully recovers to pure LCP upon entering the far-field propagation region ($z \ge z_R$), confirming spin--orbit decoupling by the nanostructure (Section~\ref{ssec:chirality_prop}).

\end{enumerate}

\section{Significance and Contributions}
\label{sec:significance}

This work addresses a previously unoccupied design space at the intersection of two active research frontiers: inverse-designed nanophotonics and valleytronics-based chiral light emission. Prior art has achieved either high LG-mode purity via passive metasurfaces or valley-locked circular polarization via TMD-integrated structures, but not both simultaneously in an ultra-compact, active platform (see Section~\ref{sec:comparison} for a quantitative comparison). The principal contribution is a verified design path that unifies active valley-polarized excitation, high-purity OAM mode shaping, and quantifiable inter-valley contrast within a single $4.5 \times 4.5\;\mu\text{m}^2$ all-dielectric footprint.

At the methodological level, the complete topology optimization workflow developed here---FDTD forward simulation, adjoint gradient computation, $\beta$-continuation binarization, and MMA-based update---is source-agnostic and mode-agnostic. It can be directly extended to other target modes (e.g., higher-order LG, Bessel, or vector beams), alternative 2D material emitters (e.g., MoSe$_2$, hBN defects), or different photonic material platforms.

At the level of physical understanding, the revealed discrepancy between modal purity and absolute coupling efficiency ($\vert{}{\rm Overlap}\vert{}^2$) as topological charge increases (Section~\ref{ssec:purity_overlap}) establishes a general design guideline: for compact high-order OAM emitters, a high modal purity does not guarantee high radiation efficiency. Therefore, both metrics must be simultaneously evaluated, and the design domain must scale with $\ell$ to maintain overall device performance.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

Building on the foundations established here, three directions offer the most immediate and high-impact extensions:

\textbf{(i) Scaling the design domain for high-order modes:} Enlarging the design area to $6.5\,\mu$m is expected to improve mode purity for $\ell = 4, 5$ to approximately $85\%$, meaningfully reducing the performance gap relative to the low-order designs while remaining within a practically feasible fabrication footprint. 

\textbf{(ii) Complete topological-charge coverage ($\ell \in [-5, 5]$):} Expanding the simulation scope to include negative orbital angular momentum modes ($\ell = -1 \text{ to } -5$) will complete the full mapping of valley-locked OAM emission, demonstrating symmetric topological-charge control enabled by the valley degree of freedom in WS$_2$.

\textbf{(iii) Low-index substrate integration for enhanced device efficiency:} Replacing standard substrates with lower-refractive-index platforms (e.g., MgF$_2$, $n \approx 1.37$) can reduce substrate radiation leakage and parasitical reflections, thereby enhancing light extraction efficiency into the targeted far-field OAM modes.
