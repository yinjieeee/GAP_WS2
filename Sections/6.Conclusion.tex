\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:summary}

This work establishes a topology-optimization-based inverse design platform for all-dielectric nanostructures,
using monolayer WS$_2$ valley-polarized excitons as the active chiral light source.
We systematically designed and verified nanostructures capable of converting a circularly polarized point source into high-purity Laguerre--Gaussian (LG) vortex beams,
covering target modes LG$_{\ell,0}$ for $\ell = 0$ through $\ell = 5$. The main results are summarized below:

\begin{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{High-purity OAM mode generation:}
    For $\ell \le 3$, the far-field LG mode purity of all optimized structures exceeds $94\%$,
    with $\ell = 1$ reaching the highest value among all six designs at $98.66\%$;
    $\ell = 4, 5$ are limited by the finite design space ($4.5\,\mu$m) and achieve mode purity of around $82\%$.
    The phase winding verification error for all six designs is below $1\%$,
    precisely confirming that the far-field vortex beams carry the correct target topological charge.

    \item \textbf{Spontaneous emergence of spiral symmetric geometry:}
    The optimized structures spontaneously form $\ell$-fold spiral symmetric geometry guided by adjoint gradients,
    physically corresponding to the continuous $\ell \times 2\pi$ phase distribution required by the LG$_{\ell,0}$ mode,
    with additional scattering compensation features superimposed at the periphery to improve mode shaping accuracy (detailed mechanism analysis in Section~\ref{ssec:spiral_emergence}).

    \item \textbf{Circular polarization selectivity verification:}
    A cross-polarization excitation test was performed on the $\ell = 3$ structure:
    under LCP excitation, the target mode purity is $94.13\%$;
    under the reverse RCP excitation, purity drops sharply to $0.84\%$ (contrast ratio $> 100:1$),
    quantitatively confirming that the optimized structure has strong circular polarization selectivity
    and can effectively distinguish the K/K$'$ dual valleys of WS$_2$.

    \item \textbf{Clarification of near-to-far-field chirality evolution mechanism:}
    Analysis of optical chirality density along the propagation axis shows that in the near field, local scattering creates a mixed LCP/RCP state;
    upon entering the far-field radiation region ($z \ge z_R$), evanescent waves decay and the propagating field fully recovers to a pure RCP chiral state,
    establishing a complete physical picture from near-field excitation to far-field OAM vortex beam (see Section~\ref{ssec:chirality_prop} for details).

\end{enumerate}

\section{Significance and Contributions}
\label{sec:significance}

The core contribution of this work is proposing and verifying a chip-scale OAM emitter design path driven by an active valley-polarized light source.
Compared to existing passive metasurface approaches relying on external plane wave excitation (e.g., Sroor 2020 achieving $\sim 90\%$ purity but without valley selectivity),
and to existing valley-OAM combination platforms (e.g., Li 2023 with valley selectivity but without quantified LG mode purity),
this work simultaneously holds competitive advantages in three dimensions: "active source + high-purity LG mode + quantifiable inter-valley contrast ratio."

At the methodological level, this work develops a complete topology optimization workflow integrating FDTD electromagnetic simulation, adjoint gradient computation, a $\beta$ continuation strategy, and the MMA solver,
using a joint logarithmic objective function of mode purity and absolute overlap integral to balance mode shape fidelity and energy coupling efficiency.
This framework is highly general and can be directly extended to other target modes, source configurations, or material systems.

At the level of physical understanding, this work reveals the decoupling mechanism of Purity and $|{\rm Overlap}|^2$ as topological charge increases,
elucidating the physical limits of high-order OAM shaping within a fixed design domain (see Section~\ref{ssec:purity_overlap}),
providing important guidance for selecting design metrics in future high-order vortex beam emitter designs.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

Based on the results of this work, the following four directions hold the most immediate value for extension:

\textbf{(i) Expand design domain and increase resolution:}
For high-order modes with $\ell \ge 4$, systematically explore the effect of enlarging the design area (target $\ge 8\,\mu$m) and increasing grid resolution on mode purity,
to break through the current $82\%$ performance ceiling.

\textbf{(ii) Dual-valley OAM multiplexing:}
Combining the K/K$'$ dual-valley selectivity of WS$_2$, design a dual-channel OAM emitter that simultaneously responds to LCP ($+\ell$) and RCP ($-\ell$) excitation,
realizing topological charge duplex encoding based on the valley degree of freedom.
The main challenge is how to simultaneously satisfy OAM shaping targets for two mutually orthogonal polarization inputs in a single structure,
which requires introducing a dual-source multi-objective optimization framework into the FOM.

\textbf{(iii) Fabrication tolerance analysis and experimental verification:}
Using the best material distribution from this work as a direct design input,
first perform FDTD fabrication tolerance simulations (sidewall roughness $\pm 5\,{\rm nm}$, etch depth $\pm 5\%$)
to evaluate the expected purity range in experiment,
then carry out GaP thin film electron beam lithography and reactive ion etching,
and measure the far-field OAM mode purity through optical excitation of WS$_2$,
completing the full research cycle from numerical prediction to experimental verification.

\textbf{(iv) Purcell cavity enhancement and valley polarization purity improvement:}
As noted in Section~\ref{ssec:current_limits}, the limited valley polarization purity of WS$_2$ at room temperature ($20$--$50\%$) is a key factor limiting actual device performance.
Combining the Purcell effect of photonic crystal microcavities or Fabry-Pérot cavities can accelerate the exciton spontaneous emission rate,
completing photon extraction before the competing depolarization mechanisms take over,
and is expected to raise the experimental valley polarization degree to $>80\%$, significantly improving the final OAM mode purity.
