    \textbf{Abstract} \\[0.5cm]

\end{center}

\normalsize
Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) naturally possess spin angular momentum (SAM) in valley excitons, while orbital angular momentum (OAM) offers unlimited degrees of freedom. Converting valley SAM into OAM selectively is key to high-capacity quantum light sources and valley photonics applications.
This study proposes a three-dimensional finite-difference time-domain (3D FDTD) inverse design framework based on the adjoint method to realize topology-optimized gallium phosphide (GaP) structures with high degrees of freedom. Within a compact volume, it converts monolayer tungsten disulfide (WS$_2$) excitons SAM into OAM with any designated topological charge, producing high-purity Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) beams.
Numerical simulations for $\ell = 0$ to $5$ prove the design optimized for left-circularly polarized emission. Far-field mode purity exceeds $80\%$. Conversely, right-circularly polarized emission drops mode purity below $1\%$, showing high valley selectivity. These results offer a new strategy for miniaturized vortex optical and valleytronic devices.
