    \textbf{Abstract} \\[0.5cm]

\end{center}

\normalsize


Valley excitons in transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) naturally carry spin angular momentum (SAM). Meanwhile, orbital angular momentum (OAM) can theoretically provide unlimited degrees of freedom. Converting valley SAM into OAM selectively is key to high-capacity quantum light sources and valley photonics applications.


This study proposes an inverse design framework combining 3D finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) with adjoint methods to design high-degree-of-freedom gallium phosphide ($\text{GaP}$) topology-optimized structure. Within a compact volume, it converts monolayer tungsten disulfide ($\text{WS}_2$) excitons SAM into OAM with any designated topological charge, producing high-purity Laguerre-Gaussian beams.

Numerical simulations for $\ell = 0$ to $5$ prove the design optimized for left-circularly polarized (LCP) emission. Far-field mode purity exceeds $80\%$. Conversely, right-circularly polarized (RCP) emission drops mode purity below $1\%$, showing high valley selectivity. These results offer a new strategy for miniaturized vortex optical and valleytronic devices.
