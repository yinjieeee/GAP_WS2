    \textbf{Abstract} \\[0.5cm]

\end{center}

\normalsize 

With the rapid advancement of valleytronics and nano-optics, manipulating the wavefront of excitonic photoluminescence from transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) at the nanoscale with high efficiency and valley selectivity has emerged as a central challenge in next-generation photonic device design.

In this thesis, we develop a high-degree-of-freedom inverse design platform for an all-dielectric gallium phosphide (GaP) nanostructure based on three-dimensional finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulation combined with the adjoint-variable method and topology optimization. The designed structure modulates the wavefront of photoluminescence from monolayer tungsten disulfide ($\text{WS}_2$) excitons to generate high-purity Laguerre--Gaussian (LG) beams carrying orbital angular momentum (OAM).

Simulation results at the $\text{WS}_2$ excitonic emission wavelength ($0.593\;\mu\text{m}$) demonstrate that, for structures optimized under left-circularly polarized (LCP) excitation corresponding to the K-valley exciton, the far-field LG mode purity exceeds $80\%$ across all six target topological charges ($\ell = 0$ to $5$), with $\ell \leq 3$ achieving purity above $94\%$ (peak: $98.66\%$ at $\ell = 1$). Phase winding analysis confirms that the topological charge error is below $1\%$ for all designs. Furthermore, when the excitation is switched to right-circularly polarized (RCP) light corresponding to the K$'$-valley exciton, the target LG mode purity drops below $1\%$, demonstrating the structure's strong circular polarization selectivity and its ability to completely distinguish the two momentum valleys of the TMD material, effectively suppressing inter-valley signal crosstalk. These results establish a new design strategy for miniaturized vortex-beam emitters and valleytronic photonic devices.
